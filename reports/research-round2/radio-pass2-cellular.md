## 2. Findings per topic

### A. 5G NR as a signal of opportunity

- A1 Abdallah, Khalife, Kassas, "Exploiting On-Demand 5G Downlink Signals for Opportunistic Navigation", IEEE SPL 30:389-393, 2023, DOI 10.1109/LSP.2023.3234496 [V full text]. Static USRP-2955, 10 MHz at 632.55 MHz (T-Mobile n71), 290 m LOS: ranging σ 5.05 m with always-on SSB only vs 2.75 m with the cognitively acquired "URS" (always-on + on-demand RS), +10 dB CNR. SSB covers 14.5-36 % of channel bandwidth and 0.0104-5.33 % duty factor depending on config.
- A2 Neinavaie, Khalife, Kassas, "Cognitive Opportunistic Navigation in Private Networks With 5G Signals and Beyond", IEEE JSTSP 16(1), 2022 [S]: UAV, 2 gNBs, 4.63 m RMSE over 416 m, RS blindly estimated.
- A3 Airbus/ESA HOP-5G: real-time 5G PRS pseudorange noise below 80 cm, three ground + one aerial (flying) BS, dedicated network [S]. NET.
- A4 Politecnico di Milano / NTT DATA / Vodafone Italia authors (first author not confirmed), "A feasibility study of 5G positioning with current cellular network deployment", Sci. Rep. 13:15281, 2023 [V full text via Europe PMC]: R&S TSMA6 scanner, 30.72 MS/s, SSB 7.2 MHz at 3.68 GHz (n78, TDD), ToA every 20 ms, 5 ns granularity; 9 cells urban (3 sites), 12 rural. gNBs synced by PTPv2 or GNSS SFP (named per PCI). Range bias per cell 73-79 m (stable) and one cell drifting 0.3 m/s; mean sync error 230 ns (~70 m); NLOS mean range error ~150 m; bias coherence 30-35 s (one cell 87 s). Position DRMS urban/rural: raw 67/35 m, bias-corrected NLS 8.2/21.4 m, corrected EKF 3.9/7.6 m (CEP95 7.9/15.3 m).
- A5 Resolution of "3.35 m": Abdallah & Kassas 2021, 5G SDR with PBCH DM-RS phase tracking, carrier phase, 3.35 m RMSE over 500 m, apparently a ground platform [S]. UCI "14.9 m": Abdallah, Shamaei, Kassas ION GNSS+ 2020, ground vehicle 1.02 km in 100 s on 5G SS only [S].
- A6 Chen et al. (Southampton), "Carrier Phase Ranging for Indoor Positioning with 5G NR Signals", IEEE IoT J 2021, arXiv 2112.11772 [S]: commercial gNB DM-RS carrier phase indoors, about 0.5 m 1σ ToA static, 95 % of pedestrian ranges within 0.8 m.
- A7 Dinh et al. (Northeastern/IMDEA), "mm-NOLOC", ACM MobiHoc 2025, DOI 10.1145/3704413.3764451 [V full text]: phone (Galaxy S21, Qualcomm modem logs) reads serving SSB beam index on Verizon/AT&T mmWave; SSB index -> angle modelled as multimodal distribution; particle filter; median < 3 m, 95th < 10 m in urban canyons; gNBs ~100 m apart. Pedestrian/vehicle at street level. No Canadian mmWave until after the 2027 auction, so sim-only.
- A8 Morrison & Sokolova (SINTEF), "Design of a custom 5G and 4G Signal of Opportunity Navigation Radio Receiver", WIPHAL'26, CEUR Vol-4298 paper 1, published 2026-10-19, CC BY 4.0 [V full text]: "Waxwing" 6 independently tunable channels, 700-6000 MHz, 7.68 MS/s-class (about 8 MHz SAW at 284.16 MHz IF) per channel, 12-bit ADC, shared 10 MHz OCXO with jitter attenuator (350 fs), USB3, 131 × 103 mm, < 300 g; first LTE capture Oct 2025; no navigation result yet. Defines what a drone-class receive-only multi-tower cellular SDR looks like.
- A9 SSB sample-rate facts [S, MathWorks/open5G_phy]: SSB detection needs 3.84 MS/s at 15 kHz SCS and 7.68 MS/s at 30 kHz SCS. [C] PSS/SSS (127 subcarriers) = 1.905 MHz at 15 kHz SCS, so an RTL-SDR at 2.4 MS/s can range on PSS/SSS of a 15 kHz-SCS carrier (e.g. n71 at 600 MHz) but not on 30 kHz-SCS n78; n78 (3.5 GHz) and n66 DL (2.1 GHz) are also above the R820T's tuning range [M]. Canadian 5G bands: n71, n66, n78 for all three national operators, plus n41 for one [S, aggregator sites].
- A10 Wuhan Univ./CUHK-SZ/SDUST, "5G NR robust tracking and positioning with GNSS assistance", Satellite Navigation, 8 Dec 2025, DOI 10.1186/s43020-025-00183-8 [S, press release only; Springer blocked]: commercial 5G NR multi-beam signals, joint SS + reference-signal tracking, phase-stabilised Kalman filter in the loop, fused position/clock fed back into 5G tracking (deep integration). No numbers obtained. Needs GNSS for assistance, so in a denial case it degrades to plain 5G tracking.
- A11 Tallinn conference paper 2025 [S]: directional SSB RSRP fingerprinting, about 20,000 measurements over 10 km², mean 26 m over a 4 km test trajectory, median 16.4 m. MDPI 2025 industrial plant: commercial sub-6 DL-AoD error < 4° for 90 % (network-side, NET) [S].
- A12 Sun, Ding, Jian, Li, Yu, "Localization Algorithm with 5G Opportunistic Signals in Co-channel Interference Environments", J. Electronics & IT 46(8):3136, 2024 [V abstract]: interference-cancellation subspace pursuit for co-channel gNBs, USRP semi-physical tests with four real gNBs at 3509.76 MHz; numbers only in figures.
- A13 Calnex deck at ATIS 2021 "Real Measurements of 5GNR Sync" [S, PDF unreachable]: SSB-based OTA time error against UTC top of second, one cell 318 ns constant with 33 ns variation, another 142 ns with 74 ns variation. Requirement ±1.5 µs (3 µs relative), ±130 ns relative TAE inside a cooperating cluster. R&S scanners measure PPS-to-SSB offset to about 30 ns.
- A14 Kassas, Abdallah, Shahcheraghi, Khalife, Lee, Jurado et al., "I Can Hear You Loud and Clear: GNSS-Less Aircraft Navigation With Terrestrial Cellular Signals of Opportunity", IEEE TAES 60(6):7694, 2024, DOI 10.1109/TAES.2024.3418943 [V full text via NSF PAR]: LTE "URS" from all antenna ports, +21 dB; aircraft over Edwards (rural) and Palmdale (semi-urban): > 100 eNodeBs trackable, some > 100 km away, at about 11,000 ft with CNR > 40 dB-Hz; carrier phase + altimeter EKF 3-D RMSE 6.8 m (43.6 km, avg 22 eNodeBs) and 8.2 m (56.8 km, avg 11 eNodeBs). Earlier C-12 data: CNR 25-55 dB-Hz at 2,000-23,000 ft AGL; two-ray model fits CNR for towers > 10 km at 16,000 ft.
- A15 CGI-led "5-GENIUS" (ESA NAVISP EL2-105), 2022 [S, webinar slides via snippet; slide PDF now 404; factsheet V but no numbers]: 5G + GNSS drone payload, flights near commercial 5G transmitters of several operators in SW London; transmitter clock models from a Kalman filter on raw RF captures; 5G pseudorange noise about 1.6 m σ; clock-model prediction about 1.3 m over 10 s; best signals at about 50 m altitude; drone-body occlusion affects visibility. Uses a Support Data Service over the internet (NET).

### B. Air-to-ground cellular ranging vs altitude

- B1 Cai, Rodríguez-Piñeiro, Yin, Ai, et al., "An Empirical Air-to-Ground Channel Model Based on Passive Measurements in LTE", IEEE TVT 68(2), 2019, DOI 10.1109/TVT.2018.2886961, arXiv 1901.07930 [V full text, AAU repository]. Passive CRS CIRs from an in-service suburban LTE eNB at 2.585 GHz; horizontal flights at 15, 30, 50, 75, 100 m (to about 500 m range) and vertical flights to about 300 m; SAGE multipath extraction. Numbers:
  - Rician K: 15 m N(12.6, 5.1) dB; 30-100 m pooled N(7.6, 5.6) dB. K is LOWER above 15 m because the drone leaves the down-tilted main beam, so LOS power drops while ground-scatter NLOS paths (lit by the main beam) stay. Vertical flights: K N(15.2, 4.7) and N(8.4, 3.8) dB, weakly rising with height (ρ 0.2-0.3).
  - RMS delay spread (log10 s): 15 m N(-7.4, 0.2) ≈ 40 ns median; 30-100 m N(-7.1, 0.3) ≈ 80 ns median [C]; vertical flights N(-6.97, 0.25) ≈ 107 ns [C], falling with height at positions near the BS. At the far end of a route above 15 m, delay spread grows (weak LOS, long NLOS delays).
  - Shadow fading N(0, 2.7) dB (horizontal) and N(0, 3.0) dB (vertical). Doppler spread falls with height (ρ -0.7).
  - Sim implication [C]: between 15 m and about 100 m the multipath does NOT get monotonically better; a 10-20 MHz LTE ranging receiver (resolution 15-30 m) will see 40-110 ns (12-33 m) delay spread unresolved, so a positive, height- and range-dependent NLOS/multipath bias of a few metres to tens of metres is realistic; model K and delay spread per this table rather than a monotone function of altitude.
- B2 Dense-urban drone campaign on a commercial n78 NR and B3 LTE network, arXiv 2411.09666 (2024), "Evaluating 5G Networks for U-Space Applications" [S]: detected neighbour PCIs rose from about 33 to 43 between 20 m and 60 m; NR mean RSRP +7 dB from ground to 20 m, then -2.7 dB by 60 m.
- B3 Helikite on commercial 3.7 GHz NR, SSBs decoded from raw IQ, RSRP/RSRQ vs altitude per PCI (arXiv 2303.01638) [S]. Austria 2020, drone to about 150 m near a commercial 5G site, 4G->5G handover about 50 m (arXiv 2004.03298) [S].
- B4 Qualcomm, "LTE Unmanned Aircraft Systems Trial Report" v1.0.1, May 2017 [V full text]: San Diego, 45 flights from ground to 120 m (400 ft), commercial PCS, AWS and 700 MHz bands, modem cell lists every about 160 ms. Detected cells rise with altitude (exceptions: 700 MHz at 30 m slightly below ground). On the ground all serving cells within 1 km and virtually all neighbours within 2 km; at 60 m > 15 % of PCS serving cells are > 5 km away. RSRP stronger at altitude despite downtilt (free-space propagation outweighs antenna-gain loss); path loss at altitude fits exponent 2.0 (ground model 3.76 with log-normal shadowing). Downlink SINR median 5 dB lower at altitude; outage (SINR < -6 dB) about 1 % in both. Implication [C]: more and farther towers are heard (better geometry, more clocks to learn), but co-channel interference lowers per-cell SINR, so per-cell C/N0 at altitude is set by interference, not path loss; tower maps must extend 5-10 km+ beyond the flight area.
- B5 Tutorial (arXiv 1901.02306) [S]: airborne receiver at 150 m and 300 m: number of cells seen rises with height; best-cell SINR far below ground. LTE test to 74 m: RSRP peaked near 34 m, RSRQ fell with height (arXiv 1903.05289) [S].
- B6 Amorim, Nguyen, Mogensen, Kovács, Wigard, Sørensen, "Radio Channel Modeling for UAV Communication Over Cellular Networks", IEEE WCL 6(4):514-517, 2017, DOI 10.1109/LWC.2017.2710045 [V full text, AAU]: live LTE 800 MHz, rural Denmark, scanner on a UAV at 1.5/15/30/60/120 m: path-loss exponent 3.7 (ground) -> 2.0 (120 m); shadowing σ 7.7 dB -> 3.4 dB; average detected cells per sample 5.1 (drive test) -> 16.9 (120 m); at about 10 km 3-D distance, 20 dB less loss at 120 m than at ground; scanner sensitivity floor rose with height (interference-limited, so even more interfering sites exist than detected). Height-dependent log10(h) model, valid 1.5-120 m. Urban follow-up (WCNC 2018, 800/1800/2600 MHz) extends to 40 m above rooftops [S].
- B7 Sidelobe effects [S]: aerial UEs are served by BS antenna sidelobes; deep nulls give abrupt signal drops and late handovers (arXiv 1804.04523; Tampere; PMC8073316). R&S drone survey: LTE coverage better at 50 ft than at 200 ft, attributed to downtilt [S, vendor blog]. Contrasts with Qualcomm (B4: stronger RSRP at altitude). Sim implication [C]: per-tower received power vs elevation should include a vertical antenna pattern with nulls (e.g. 3GPP 65°/10° pattern with downtilt) so that individual towers drop out in elevation bands, not just a smooth path-loss law.
- B8 Korean urban LTE study (Sensors, PMC6806180) [S]: at 150 ft over a dense urban LTE network, LTE alone gives 15-20 m horizontal error; Android network location on drones 32-38 m (urban/suburban) and 1,637 m (rural) CEP68 (already in radio.md). AERPAW RSRP vs altitude fits a two-ray model once the measured antenna pattern is included (arXiv 2307.12515) [S].
- B9 Vertical geometry [S, arXiv 2604.08734 intro]: towers and drones at similar heights give nearly planar geometry, so TOA-only altitude is poor; barometer needed (we already have one).
- B10 Cai et al., "Empirical Low-Altitude Air-to-Ground Spatial Channel Characterization for Cellular Networks Connectivity", IEEE JSAC 39(10):2975, 2021, arXiv 2010.00841 [S]: 16-element UCA, two LTE networks, 0-40 m, rural/urban/industrial; reported that K-factor can fall with height because strong building reflections get blocked. 3.565 GHz 5G ground-to-air (IEEE Access 2024, DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3469050) found K rising with altitude [S] (already in radio.md as a snippet). Both directions are real; it depends on whether the drone is in the main beam.
- B11 Dense-urban campaign detail (arXiv 2411.09666) [S]: from 20 to 60 m, RSRP fell 2.7 dB (NR n78) and 4.2 dB (LTE B3) while PCIs heard rose about 33 -> 43.
- B12 del Peral-Rosado, López-Salcedo, Zanier, Seco-Granados, "Position Accuracy of Joint Time-Delay and Channel Estimators in LTE Networks", IEEE Access 6:25185, 2018 [V abstract]: periodic-tap joint ML delay/channel estimator, about 10 m position accuracy at 10 MHz in challenging urban channels (simulation + lab), and cites skew-t ranging-error models fitted to LTE lab measurements. Ground, not air.
- Still not found (B): any altitude-binned pseudorange/TOA error distribution at 30-150 m from a real network. The closest are delay spread/K-factor by height (B1, B6) and noise-only σ (radio.md: Maaref & Kassas 0.75/1.23 m).

### C. Radio SLAM, mapper/navigator, tower maps

- C1 Nguyen, Shadram, Kassas, "A Lower Bound for the Error Covariance of Radio SLAM with Terrestrial Signals of Opportunity", ION GNSS+ 2021 [V full text via NSF PAR 10413008]: observability of the base case (one unknown tower + two towers of known position but unknown clocks) after l ≥ 4 epochs; EKF covariance lower bound. UAV flight: 175 s without GNSS, 600 m, 2-D RMSE 10.76 m; the unknown tower's 2-D position error fell from 41.57 m to 6.62 m. Gives a realistic UAV-scale convergence number: a tower prior off by ~40 m converges to ~7 m within one short flight given two known towers.
- C2 ISED site data now carries cell identities and antenna geometry [V]. ISED "Decision on Amendments to Requirements for Spectrum Licensees to Submit Technical Information about Sites", DGSO-004-23, Aug 2023 (https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/node/2009): coordinates in decimal degrees with at least 4 decimal places (up to 7); monthly update and confirmation required; adds Cell ID and Physical Cell ID fields for 3GPP technologies; ISED itself cites "a growing need to substantially improve data quality, reliability and uniformity" and industry concerns about reliability (no error rates given). The public "Terrestrial Spectrum Licence Site Data Extract - Field Descriptions" (update Oct 2024) [V] lists columns: Radio Technology, Cell Identification, Physical Cell Identification, Latitude/Longitude (WGS84), Structure Height, TX antenna height, TX horizontal and vertical beamwidth, TX azimuth, TX elevation angle (tilt), TX gain, line loss. 
  - [C] 4 decimal places = 11 m in latitude and about 8 m in longitude at 45° N, so the registry's quantisation alone is ~±5 m; actual entry errors unknown (TBD). 
  - Implication: if the PCI/Cell ID columns are populated for Ottawa (TBC, not checked; would need the extract, owner go-ahead), the heard PCI can be associated to a registered antenna directly, and azimuth/tilt/beamwidth give the vertical pattern for the altitude model in topic B. This is the single most useful map source found. Vendor critique (Loxcel) says ISED data quality is inadequate and cleans duplicates and unit mismatches [S, interested party].
- C3 Brunello et al., "Crowdsourced Reconstruction of Cellular Networks to Serve Outdoor Positioning", Sensors 23(1):352, 2022, PMC9823457 [V full text, two-thirds read]: OpenCelliD (42.95 M measurements) + u-blox (12.5 M) used to reconstruct cell coverage; no tower-position error against ground truth reported. UK ONS working paper questions OpenCelliD accuracy [S]; an informal test got a 35 km "accuracy" from the OpenCelliD API for an unknown cell [S]. So no published metre-level OpenCelliD vs surveyed-mast comparison exists; radio.md's 40-100 m (timing-advance/drive-test) stays the best number.
- C4 Chen, Li, Whiton, Leitinger, Tufvesson, "Robust Localization in Modern Cellular Networks Using Global Map Features", arXiv 2509.10433 / IEEE OJSP 7:356-372, 2026 [V, experiment section of HTML]: LTE multipath-based SLAM (virtual anchors from reflections) with a PHD filter re-using map features from earlier passes. Real test in Lund: USRP-2953R on a rubidium/GPSDO reference, 128-port switched circular array on a car roof, 2.66 GHz, 20 MHz, two eNBs (3 cells each) with known positions, 1,750 m in 32.5 min (4 laps): RMSE odometry 6.51 m, MP-SLAM 3.69 m, with global map features 2.91 m (USRP's own GPS 6.73 m). Not drone-portable (128-port array, rubidium), but shows reflections as extra anchors once tower positions are known.
- C5 Upgrade of radio.md topic 10 (Kassas et al., T-ITS 25(10):14164, 2024) to [V full text, NSF PAR 10652797]: tower prior covariance 1e5 m² per horizontal axis (σ ≈ 316 m) and 1e2 m² vertical. Tower 2-D errors after radio SLAM on the C-12: 263.65 -> 19.38 m; 298.98 -> 29.54 m; 134.57 -> 31.02 m; 71.14 -> 17.59 m. True positions taken from Google Earth. So a tower prior of a few hundred metres converges to about 20-30 m on an aircraft with industrial IMU over minutes; it does not reach the ~5-7 m of the UAV base case (C1), because the aircraft geometry is far and fast.
- C6 Public-registry accuracy anecdotes [S, trade press]: FCC expects about 1 arc-second (≈30 m) but has no numeric rule; an antenna found about 1,000 ft (300 m) from its registered point; NAD27/NAD83 datum mixing is a common cause. The Kassas group itself maps eNodeBs from Google Earth and calls it "not very accurate" [S, patent]. Sim implication [C]: tower-position prior σ about 10-30 m for a registry entry, with a small fraction (TBD, a few %) of gross errors of 100-300 m.
- C7 Sel, Hayek, Kassas (OSU), "Robust Position Estimation using Range Measurements from Transmitters with Inaccurate Positions", conference paper, about 2024-2025, NSF PAR 10652786 [V full text]; companion worst-case bound paper NSF PAR 10652791 [S]. Min-max estimator with each tower inside a disk of radius ε; offline "sensitivity map" of position error by region. With ε = 245 m (95 % of σ = 100 m per axis), snapshot range-only positioning gives 105-182 m RMSE (sim) and 114-195 m (real vehicle, 7 Verizon/T-Mobile towers, GPS jammed, USRP on GNSSDO, clocks removed with truth). Rule of thumb [C]: without SLAM or IMU, receiver error is about the size of the tower-position error; the map must be good to ~10 m to keep LTE at the 10 m level.
- C8 Mozilla Location Service retired in stages from 13 Mar 2024 (repo archived 31 Jul 2024); beaconDB is a community successor (Wi-Fi dumps public domain; cell coverage unclear) [S]. So MLS is no longer a live tower source; OpenCelliD terms not checked this pass.
- C9 Radio-fingerprint SLAM with phones on a robot (arXiv 2305.13635, 2023) [S]: LTE cell-ID similarity loop closures 30.25 m mean (σ 22.1 m) vs Wi-Fi 3.79 m. LTE fingerprints are tens-of-metres class even on the ground.

- C11 Late additions [S]: a Kassas-group patent on lane-level ground-vehicle navigation with lidar + cellular reports a third tower's initial position error of 476.31 m falling to 10.56 m after a mapping mode and below 5 m after SLAM (lidar-aided, ground). Ulm et al. (IEEE 2015) found OpenCelliD-based localisation errors fat-tailed, validated with GPS/cell-ID traces from 250 people. Supports the "few % gross errors" term in the tower prior.

### E. Cell tower clocks

- E1 PRIMARY SOURCE READ for the Edwards observation: Kassas, Khalife, Abdallah, Lee, "I Am Not Afraid of the Jammer: Navigating with Signals of Opportunity in GPS-Denied Environments", ION GNSS+ 2020, St. Louis [V full text, NSF PAR 10225276]; later IEEE AES Magazine 37(7), 2022, DOI 10.1109/MAES.2022.3154110. Facts:
  - DT NAVFEST, Edwards AFB, Sep 2019; six high-power jammers + one portable jammer over an area about 50 miles north of Edwards; J/S "as high as 90 dB" overall, but **J/S at the two eNodeBs was about 60 dB**.
  - **Only GPS was jammed; Galileo and GLONASS were not** (stated in the paper). The authors themselves say the stability "could be attributed to either (i) the oscillators equipped on the towers are disciplined by other GNSS constellations or (ii) the free-running oscillators remained stable".
  - The two eNodeBs were **co-located at the same site**, from two operators (Verizon 751/2125 MHz, cell ID 377; T-Mobile 731.5/2145 MHz, cell ID 491). Jammers cycled 10 min on, 2 min off. Stationary receiver outside the jammed area (GPSDO), 10 MS/s, 95 min, with a 5-min data loss at about minute 35.
  - Metric: difference of the two pseudoranges (clock-bias difference) "hovering around 1835 m", no visible correlation with jamming windows; plotted, not quantified (no Allan deviation, no drift number).
  - Mobile test: one eNodeB heard; 130 s GPS outage; radio SLAM 2-D RMSE 29.4 m with tower known, 32.2 m with tower initialised 1.07 km off (±3σ 3.3 km).
  - So this is NOT evidence of tower holdover behaviour: towers could have stayed locked to Galileo/GLONASS, and the pair shared a site. It supports only "multi-constellation-disciplined co-sited towers stay mutually stable under GPS-only jamming". The model in the first report (16.3: shared onset, per-tower drift afterwards) remains untested by any public measurement.
- E2 Upgrade to [V full text, PMC10781334]: Winter, Morrison, Sokolova, "Analysis of 5G and LTE Signals for Opportunistic Navigation and Time Holdover", Sensors 24(1):213, 2024. USRP B200/B210 on a GNSS-disciplined Rb (Safran LNRClok-1500, 50 ns spec); 14 cells of three operators (Telenor 7, Telia 4, ICE 3), 465-2680 MHz, **all FDD** (no TDD in Norway). One Telenor cell (2660 MHz) tracked about 10 h with **about 100 ns stability** and a long-term time-error rate of **about 0.03 ppb**; within 1 h under 1.25 m (≈4 ns) p-p; ICE cell > 1,200 ns per hour with discontinuous phase (slope corners, long pauses between steering updates). Same-operator pair 1.25 m p-p vs cross-operator 3.5 m p-p over 1 h; but the cross-operator pair had better Allan deviation below 9 s, and cell locations were not public, so "same-operator towers share a reference" is still not shown (the pair may be co-sited). Zero-baseline check under 2 cm. Suggested tracking update rate about 100 Hz (Allan knee near 10 ms).
- E3 Requirement values, upgraded from [M] to [S] (itecspec copy of TS 36.104 §6.5.3.1): TAE 65 ns between MIMO/Tx-diversity ports per carrier; 130 ns intra-band contiguous CA; 260 ns intra-band non-contiguous and inter-band CA. NR TS 38.104: same ±65 ns MIMO and 260 ns inter-band CA per an arXiv summary [S]. One patent cites 1.3 µs for inter-band CA (older release or different scenario) [S].
- E4 SINTEF Jammertest 2022 timing note [S, SINTEF publication 2210272/2369706]: multi-band multi-constellation timing receivers can lose time sync when only a subset of their bands is jammed, and some drift rapidly when the constellation chosen for the time scale is denied for long. Relevance [C]: a tower's GNSS timing receiver may misbehave under partial (e.g. L1-only) jamming instead of cleanly entering holdover; a sim fault worth adding as "clock step or fast drift at jamming onset" for some towers (TBC). Cisco Live 2024 (BRKSPG-3050) [S]: with a local oscillator, staying under 1.1 µs is possible only for "several hours".
- A16 Barbieri, Brambilla, Goia, Nicoli, Capone, "An Experimental Analysis on the Readiness of Currently-Deployed 5G Networks for Positioning", IEEE Trans. Instrum. Meas., 2025 [V abstract, Polimi IRIS 11311/1294170; full text restricted]: four Milan operators, n78, passive SSB ToA + power, urban and rural; ToA accuracy "significantly varies" by area and operator; synchronisation "strong issues". This is very probably the "±60 m ranging error from synchronisation instabilities" source cited by the Polimi IEEE Access 2026 paper (radio.md 4.3) [C, inference from authors and wording].
- A17 Pileggi, Grec, Biagi, "5G Positioning: An Analysis of Early Datasets", Sensors 23(22):9222, 2023 [V full text]: ESA GINTO5G private 5G network (Fraunhofer IIS, 11 gNBs, 3.7 GHz, 100 MHz, USRP X300): after inter-gNB calibration, TDoA LS mean 2-D errors 0.6-0.9 m outdoors, worst cases 19-27 m, < 10 blunders > 100 m. NET (private network with calibrated sync); shows the bandwidth ceiling, not what a commercial n78 SSB gives (A4: 3.9-7.6 m with correction, 35-67 m raw).
- D4 Tower selection under limited receiver channels [S]: Nguyen (UC Irvine MS thesis 2022, "Aerial Vehicle Navigation with Terrestrial Signals of Opportunity: Performance Analysis and Transmitter Selection", eScholarship 2gf6j0c2); Nguyen & Kassas ION ITM 2022 (OSTI 2001775) and IEEE AES Magazine Oct 2023: an aerial vehicle hearing M towers (example M = 57) can track only K < M; opportunistic greedy selection (OGS) and one-shot selection (OSS) approximate the optimal information-gathering subset. Directly relevant to RTL-SDR-class payloads that can tune only one carrier per dongle: the sim should model a channel budget and a selection policy, not "all towers heard are used".
- B13 The one altitude-binned real ranging result found. Dickerson, Masrur, Dickerson, Özdemir, Güvenç, "Impact of Altitude, Bandwidth, and NLOS Bias on TDOA-Based 3D UAV Localization: Experimental Results and CRLB Analysis", arXiv 2502.01771, Feb 2025 (ICC 2025 workshop) [V, HTML Table II]. NSF AERPAW (rural NC), the UAV transmits a 3.32 GHz sounding waveform (TX, so the geometry is reversed; the channel is reciprocal [C]), four Keysight N6841A ground sensors (one NLOS throughout). Average 3-D error with outliers removed / valid-measurement rate: 40 m: 79.1 m @1.25 MHz, 55.9 m @2.5 MHz, 48.9 m @5 MHz (73-76 % valid); 70 m @5 MHz: 46.9 m (84 % valid); 100 m @5 MHz: 35.8 m (92 % valid). Totals including outliers 164-359 m. NLOS bias not quantified.
  - Sim implication [C]: at 5 MHz-class bandwidth (close to LTE PSS/SSS's ~1 MHz or a narrow LTE carrier), the fraction of usable measurements rises from ~75 % at 40 m to ~92 % at 100 m and the outlier-free error falls ~25 % between 40 and 100 m; outliers are gross (hundreds of m) and frequent, so the filter needs innovation gating; the 40-100 m trend is real but modest, and bandwidth matters more than altitude in this range.
- B14 Dataset (owner go-ahead needed): "AERPAW Keysight N6841A RF sensor UAV localization measurements with LOS/NLOS information, July 2024", Dryad DOI 10.5061/dryad.vq83bk44h (Mar 2025 version) [S]: five flights, UAV position estimates, ground truth, per-sensor LOS/NLOS flags, error vs altitude. Licence not read (Dryad normally CC0, TBC). Could calibrate an altitude-dependent NLOS/outlier model.
- C10 Upgrade of radio.md topic 3/10 UniBw entry [V slides, PLANS 2023, Hameed, Arizabaleta-Diez, Philips-Blum, Pany]: mapping an LTE transmitter (institute-run Amarisoft eNB, so no inter-PCI clock bias or MIMO effects) from a DJI M600 Pro with a 6-state KF [pos, float ambiguity, clock bias, drift] and receiver clock from filtered GNSS SPP (1st-order polynomial): code + carrier gives the transmitter position within 0.3 m (horizontal) and within 1 m vertical for the best of three flights; the SPP receiver clock is the biggest error source. And: the Google Maps coordinate of that antenna was off by [-29.71, -6.11, -33.78] m (E/N/U) from the surveyed truth, i.e. about 30 m horizontally and 34 m vertically. (A search summary claiming an "on-board atomic clock" was wrong; the slides say GNSS SPP.) NET-free but needs GNSS during mapping.
- E5 A real network-wide timing fault [S, Inside GNSS / RNT Foundation / Chronos ITSF 2016 deck]: 26 Jan 2016, a GPS ground-software upload while removing SVN 23 put a ~13 µs error into the UTC message of 15 satellites for about 12 h; UK telecom sync supply units saw the 13 µs error, disqualified GPS and went into holdover across many sites; alarms took until 28 Jan to clear. No LTE-specific timing data published. Sim relevance [C]: a GNSS time fault or time spoof can hit every GNSS-timed tower at once (a common-mode step up to µs = km, or a holdover start), which a tower-clock model with only independent random walks cannot represent; a shared step at onset should be a fault-injection option.

- E6 Holdover component figures [S, vendor]: plain OCXOs hold 1.5 µs for roughly 2-12 h; PPS-disciplined, compensated OCXOs 24-48 h; temperature error grows linearly and ageing quadratically with time (ATIS WSTS decks). Consistent with radio.md's holdover table; no base-station-vendor figure found.

## 5. Judgement per topic

- **A. 5G NR.** Close to exhausted for what exists publicly: commercial-network SSB results are ground-only and sit at 4-20 m with a reference receiver, ±60-450 m raw; the one UAV result is from a 2-gNB experiment in 2022. More searching would mostly re-find the Kassas and Polimi lines. For Ottawa the practical question is hardware (n71 is the only 5G band an RTL-SDR can tune, and only PSS/SSS fit), which needs a capture, not a paper.
- **B. Altitude.** The specific downlink statistic still does not exist in the open literature; I now have a defensible model from channel campaigns (B1, B4, B6) and one altitude-binned TDOA result (B13). Further searching has low value; the AERPAW datasets (B14 and the first report's 16.1) and our own flights are the way to calibrate it.
- **C. Radio SLAM / maps.** Methods and convergence numbers are in hand. The highest-value open item is not a paper: checking whether ISED's public extract carries PCI, azimuth and tilt for Ottawa sites (C2), which needs the owner's go-ahead to download.
- **D. Low-cost LTE.** The only comparable result (BYU 8.03 m) is behind 403/paywall on every route tried; ask the owners whether someone can obtain the PDF through a library. Nothing else in this class has been published; more searching will not change that.
- **E. Tower clocks.** The key correction is that the Edwards observation is not holdover evidence. No public measurement of tower clocks under GNSS loss exists; the best remaining evidence would come from a capture at a jamming event or from an operator. More web searching is unlikely to help.
