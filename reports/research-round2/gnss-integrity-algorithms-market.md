## Topic 1 notes 

- Kriezis, Chen, Akos, Lo, Walter, "Real-World Spoofing Detection and Characterization Using Low-Cost Receivers", ION ITM 2025 (Stanford PDF). Two u-blox F9P (L1/L2 and L1/L5) on a ship to Haifa, summer 2024. [V] Spoofer was "advanced" (real-time updated sims), so ephemeris checks failed; C/N0 was the useful metric because wide-area spoofers are unusually strong; pseudorange jumped 2267 km in seconds; receiver clock bias jumped ~2400 km equivalent at spoof onset; L5 stayed genuine so the clock anomaly showed on the L5 solution. DFMC SBAS MOPS RFI detector (C/N0 + satellite count, 27 dB-Hz availability threshold): 94 % accuracy, 4 % false positives on the L1/L5 unit but "coincidental" (receiver dropped L1 sats inconsistent with L5); under 13 % on the L1/L2 unit. Conclusion: MOPS detector ineffective for most cases.
- Blanch, Lo, Chen, Kriezis, Walter, "Protection Levels Against Spoofing Using Dual Antennas: a Practical Approach", ION GNSS+ 2025 (Stanford PDF). [V] Weighted-sum-of-squared-residuals statistic across two antennas, chi-square with p(n-1)-3 dof nominal, non-central under spoof; protection level over all capture hypotheses; extended to unknown orientation; carrier-phase double differences go to zero modulo wavelength under full capture. Tested on Jammertest Sept 2024 data. No Pd/Pfa table in the paper.
- Johansson, Spanghero, Papadimitratos, "Consumer INS Coupled with Carrier Phase Measurements for GNSS Spoofing Detection", arXiv 2502.03870 (2025). [V abstract] Short-time inertial integration of antenna displacement correlated with carrier-phase variation; single antenna; phone-grade MEMS as good as industrial at short integration; field test at Jammertest 2024; up to 90 % correct classification. No Pfa or latency in abstract.
- Kriezis et al., "GNSS Jamming and Spoofing Monitoring Using Low-Cost COTS Receivers", arXiv 2509.13600 (2025, rev. 2026, NAVIGATION submission). [V abstract] C/N0 vs calibrated received power 2-D space separates nominal/jammed/spoofed/blocked; Norway controlled tests plus Poland and SE Mediterranean deployments. [S] AGC and C/N0 saturate at relatively low jamming powers; thresholds must be calibrated per receiver.
- Vendor flags: u-blox's own Jammertest 2025 write-up says both jamming and spoofing flags rose during jamming-only runs [S]; German BSH report finds u-blox spoof flag mechanism unclear and conservative [S]; GPSPatron could not trigger the M8T spoof flag in its tests [S]; u-blox manuals: detection does not work in single-GNSS mode or if the receiver starts under spoofing [S].
- Wesson et al. power-distortion detector: alarms on all attacks, <0.6 % single-channel false alarm over >900,000 tests (TEXBAT etc.) [S] (arXiv 1702.06554).
- SQM on TEXBAT: existing metrics ROC AUC 0.82 and 0.76 on TEXBAT cases 2 and 3 [S]; PFA often not reported, so cross-paper comparison is unreliable [S].
- PX4 EKF2 innovation-gate spoofing: Jung et al., IEEE Access 2024: a slow spoof stays under the innovation test ratio; random Kalman-gain scaling made it detectable (sim + driver-injected "physical" test, not over the air) [S]. arXiv 2512.20964: attacks that keep the PX4 test ratio low [S]. Community SITL claims (96 %, <120 ms) are unreviewed [S].
- Clock-bias methods: Truong et al., Sensors 2023 (low-cost receiver clock bias; simulator + moving target; ROC) [S]; SCV-RCS clock bias vs integrated drift self-consistency, Sensors 2026 [S]; OSTI (Sandia/ORNL?) simulation: innovation monitoring lowest detection, Doppler-based highest; CSAC/Rb clocks raise false alerts of clock-jump tests [S].
- Rothmaier et al., "GNSS spoofing detection through spatial processing", NAVIGATION 2021 (DOI 10.1002/navi.420): dual-polarisation antenna, flight data on a C-12; missed detection >50 % lower than prior methods at fixed Pfa [S]. Already in round 1 via 68/2/243 (47/129 epochs).
- No published UAV flight test with over-the-air spoofing reporting Pd, Pfa per hour and time to detect for any GNSS/IMU consistency method was found (8 searches). Closest: Jammertest 2024 ground/vehicle work above; VTOL jammer-localisation flights (round 1).

## Topic 2 notes 
- Oligeri, Sciancalepore, Di Pietro, "GNSS Spoofing Detection via Opportunistic IRIDIUM Signals", arXiv 2006.10284 (WiSec 2020). [V] Position from Iridium Ring Alert beam data: ~10 km error after ~6,100 IRA messages; collection time ~6 min at 1 % packet loss, ~1 h at 10 %, ~10 h at their 99 % loss (balcony, wrong antenna, USRP X310, gr-iridium). Alarm thresholds 10/15/20 km. Only coarse (10 km) spoof detection; one sample over threshold triggers.
- Liu, Papadimitratos, "Extending RAIM with a Gaussian Mixture of Opportunistic Information", arXiv 2402.03449 (2024). [V abstract] Positions from GNSS subsets and wireless (cellular/Wi-Fi) subsets, motion-filtered with onboard sensors, fused into composite likelihood; real dataset Kista (Stockholm); detectable when number of benign subsets exceeds a threshold; attacker may also control Wi-Fi/cellular. 
- Liu, Papadimitratos, "GNSS Spoofing Detection Based on Opportunistic Position Information" (PADS), arXiv 2506.12580 (2025). [V abstract] Weighted polynomial regression on positions with velocity/acceleration constraints, GP uncertainty, outlier ensemble; up to 3x true positive rate at low false-positive rate vs SOP-only/inertial/statistical baselines. No absolute Pd/Pfa in abstract. Also "Probabilistic detection of GNSS spoofing using opportunistic information" arXiv 2305.05404 [S].
- Padova thesis "GNSS spoofing detection techniques by cellular network cross-check in smartphones": compares GNSS with serving base-station position and with network position; Android app [S].
- Khalife, Maaref, Kassas, "Opportunistic Autonomous Integrity Monitoring for Enhanced UAV Safety", IEEE AES Magazine 2023 (OSU PDF). [V] ARAIM extended to LTE SOPs on a UAV; fault tree with P(single SOP fault) = 1e-4 (proposed from campaign; GPS 1e-5), SOP constellation fault 0 (they flag this as possibly optimistic); sigma_URA,SOP = 1.5 x sigma_URE,SOP; b_nom,SOP = 0.75 m (borrowed from GPS); base station with LOS to towers removes common errors; PL-to-error gap cut >55 % (V) and 70 % (H) vs GPS-only; GPS-only ARAIM missed injected GPS faults that OARAIM caught. Authors say results are not enough to generalise. NET for the base station.
- Time cross-checks: Spanghero/Papadimitratos time-offset validation (arXiv 2202.10891, 2202.10838) uses network time incl. LTE SIB16 GPS time; risk that attacker also controls network time [S]. 3GPP has no alarm types for timing anomalies (arXiv 2607.11398, 2026) [S]. No shipping product found that checks GNSS time against LTE/5G base stations.
- Products: Ground Control RockBLOCK APNT (Iridium PNT) flagged GNSS divergence within 1 s at Jammertest 2026 (vendor) [S] (round 1 had this). High Lander + BOND RF (Oct 2026): interference detect/classify/localise fed to UTM, ground-side [S]. GPSPatron GP-Probe: multi-antenna DOA ground probe; at Jammertest 2024 self-reported median 0 s, mean 0.6 s latency, 100 % interference detection [S].
- ATSC 3.0 Broadcast Positioning System (BPS, US TV): 15-20 ns timing at NAB 2025 demo (vendor) [S]; NIST TDEV vs GPS <4 ns [S]; USDOT US$744k field-test contract Oct 2025 [S]; four-tower multilateration prototype planned 2026-27 [S]. Not in Canada (ATSC 1.0 here) [M].
- DME/APNT cross-check concept: URI/USCG paper notes position comparison with DME-DME/R-Mode/SoOP "looks easy but is subtle" [S]; patent US11467290 compares GNSS bearing/range with DME [S].
- Doppler-frequency metric for spoofing (PolyU, Aerospace Systems 2026): on TEXBAT, detection delay <5 s at ~2 % false alarm [S].
- No paper found using Starlink/LEO Doppler as a GNSS spoof cross-check (2 searches); LEO only appears as navigation aid.

## Topic 3 notes 
- OSNMA TTFAF: Galan-Figueras et al., "Improving Galileo OSNMA Time To First Authenticated Fix", arXiv 2403.14739, IEEE TAES Oct 2025 [S]: hot start ~100 s typical; optimised mean 60.9 s (test vectors), 68.8 s open sky, best 44.0 s; urban 128->88 s (soft), 266->146 s (hard) [S, secondary]; optimisations assume tighter time sync than the receiver guidelines; open-source OSNMAlib.
- OSNMA attacks: Wang et al., "Practical Spoofing Attacks against Galileo OSNMA with Time-Synchronization Manipulation", arXiv 2501.09246 (v2 Aug 2026). [V abstract] TS-compliant replay, forgery and dual-frequency forgery all passed OSNMA on two commercial receivers and two open SDR receivers and moved them to attacker-chosen positions/times. [S] Exploits the allowed gap between local time and Galileo System Time; official updates may have closed some.
- Inside GNSS on Jammertest 2023: OSNMA flagged synthesized nav bits, but gave a green light during meaconing or spoofing with true broadcast ephemerides (no time-consistency check) [S].
- Galileo SAS (E6-C encrypted ranging): E6-C encryption testing from 2025-12-03; first civil authenticated position 2026-09-17 (GSC news); all-constellation E6-C encryption 2H 2026; Initial Service Declaration early 2027 (EUSPA 2026 slides) [S]. ESA report: SAS receiver kept the correct position while conventional receivers were spoofed at Jammertest [S].
- GPS CHIMERA: NTS-3 launched (L3Harris one-year retrospective Sept 2026 lists high-power signals, agile acquisition, flexible messages; no CHIMERA result named) [S]; IETF draft (Aug 2026) on CHIMERA marker key packages says an adaptation is proposed for a future GPS civil authentication service [S].
- "GNSS SpAmming" (arXiv 2602.05517, 2026): spoofing-based denial of service via CDMA; especially effective on cold-started receivers [V abstract].
- Multi-band under jamming: no independent availability numbers found. u-blox Jammertest 2025 slides: ZED-X20P under CW/swept/PRN on L1,G1,L2,L5,E6 incl. 50 W PRN "pyramid"; vendor conclusion that L2/L5/E5/E6 redundancy kept position [S]. MFMC software receiver, Gulf of Finland (Sensors 2022, 22/6/2294): switches to unjammed bands [S]. oneNav L5-direct (Haifa, July 2024): kept fixes while phones and Garmin watches failed (vendor, no powers or runs given) [S].
- Vector tracking / deep coupling with low-cost IMU: no measured dB jamming margin with a MEMS IMU 2023-26 found; one MEMS deep-coupling study could not bridge 35 s of jamming [S]. Round-1 7-15 dB figure stands as the only number.

- Spoof mitigation that recovers the true signal: SemperFi (Sathaye, LaMountain, Closas, Ranganathan, NDSS 2022, arXiv 2105.01860) single-antenna, GNSS-SDR based, adversarial peak identifier + SIC; recovers true location within 0.54 s on Jetson Xavier after flight patterns <100 m (NDSS; v1 said <50 m and 10 s) [S]. Stanford secondary-peak tracking + multi-hypothesis KF (Rothmaier ION ITM 2021) tracked both spoofed and authentic solutions in two scenarios [S]. - Small arrays, independent tests: none found for drones. FEMTO-ST (Friedt et al., GRCon 2020): Raspberry Pi 4 + B210 + gnss-sdr, controlled-pattern antenna null-steering toward one source, demonstrated on GPS L1 (no numbers in abstract) [V abstract]. NAVSYS 1999: shrinking a CRPA's spacing by 35 % limits null depth to ~4 dB (model) [S].
- FocalPoint "Supercorrelation" spoof defence at Jammertest 2025 (vendor blog) [S].

## Topic 4 notes 
- Navigating off a jammer: Tedeschi, Oligeri, Di Pietro, "Leveraging Jamming to Help Drones Complete Their Mission" (JAM-ME), IEEE Access 8, 2020 (CC BY 4.0). [S] Drone estimates jammer position from RSS, then uses it as a radio beacon to reach a target inside the jammed area; simulation only, with wind drift; ~70 % path overhead under conservative assumptions, "at least tenfold" less in practice. No metre error found in snippets. Patent by the same author on jamming localisation supporting navigation [S].
- Kassas group, "I am not afraid of the GPS jammer" (IEEE AES Magazine 2022?, OSU PDF): Edwards AFB, GPS jammed up to J/S 90 dB; ground vehicle, ~5 km in 180 s, 32 m RMSE using one LTE SOP with poorly known position (plus IMU?) [S]. Not jammer-as-beacon; navigating through jamming.
- Stanford JAGER: multirotor with rotating directional antenna, bearings to jammer by max-RSSI/cross-correlation; aims at simultaneous jammer and vehicle localisation (SLAM) [S]. No accuracy figure found.
- Virginia Tech UAS localiser (NSF PAR 10497901): fly-by power, sequential NLS, 868 MHz LoRa surrogate because live GNSS jamming is illegal; metre-level achievable when antenna gain pattern over time is modelled [S].
- arXiv 2405.07611 (2024): VTOL with consumer GNSS receiver heat-maps interference; relative heading and position of source within tens of metres [S]. (arXiv 2503.20352, few metres, was in round 1.)
- Liu, Lo, Blanch, Chen, Walter, "Locating GNSS Interference Sources using ADS-B with Non-linear Least Squares", NAVIGATION 72(3), Sept 2025. [V] NIC->received power (NIC 0 ~ above -115 dBW; NIC 1-6 ~ -120 to -115 dBW), Friis model, weighted Gauss-Newton; Denver Jan 2022 event, one hour of ADS-B: estimate ~4 km from the true jammer, 95 % region ~10 km radius; power off by 30 W.
- Spoofer localisation from ADS-B: line-of-sight circle overlap (Stanford pipeline, daily worldwide) [S]; Jansen et al. track geometry + multilateration: spoofer within a search radius of a few km (preliminary) [S].
- ADS-B interference maps (GPSJAM etc.) only see high-power interference; ADS-B has no direct interference metric [S]; Pleninger et al., Aeronautical Journal 2024: NACp + almanac method validated on deliberate jamming tests [S].
- Route planning out of jamming: GCN multi-UAV jamming-area prediction (arXiv 2405.00689), Bayesian active inference online jammer inference + trajectory (arXiv 2512.05711), decentralised DRL (arXiv 2104.04477) [S]; "escape time" with sensor drift (arXiv 1906.05348) [S]. Most optimisation methods assume jammer known. No flight result found.
- Canada: ISED PNT Office ran a 2022 study for a national GNSS monitoring/reporting service (outcome not found) [S]; NAV CANADA jammer detection and geolocation reported to ICAO 2018 [S]; Transport Canada 2024 CASA asks operators to report GNSS interference [S]; Canadian oceanic airspace Jan-Jun 2025: 255 flights with ADS-B anomalies, 447 with ADS-B and ADS-C anomalies (ICAO EUR/NAT 2025 slides) [S].

## Topic 5 notes 
- KEY: Jia, Khalife, Kassas, "Performance Analysis of Opportunistic ARAIM for Navigation With GNSS Signals Fused With Terrestrial Signals of Opportunity", IEEE T-ITS 24(10):10587-10602, Oct 2023 (DOI 10.1109/TITS.2023.3277393; OSU PDF). [V] Adding ONE SOP increases both HPL and VPL; happens when only one SOP exists or SOP clocks are not synchronised (each SOP its own "constellation"): the new clock state absorbs the measurement, so geometry improves but redundancy does not, and a new fault mode is added. Two or more SOPs (sharing a clock) reduce PLs. Even with P_fault,new = 0, one measurement from a new constellation increases VPL. UAV experiment, GPS + cellular SOPs, 2880 epochs, 100 % normal operation on Stanford diagram; one SOP: HPE unchanged, HPL up; >=2 SOPs: both down. Upgrades round-1 16.7 from snippet to checked and supplies the mechanism.
- Langel, Garcia Crespillo, Joerger, "Overbounding the effect of uncertain Gauss-Markov noise in Kalman filtering", NAVIGATION 68(2):259-276, 2021 (DOI 10.1002/navi.419). [V abstract page] When the true GM sigma and tau are only known within ranges, model the GM state with tau_max; initialise its variance at a derived minimum (Eq. 25), use their non-stationary model (Eq. 28 discrete). [S, MITRE ION 2019]: KF covariance overbounds when GM is defined with tau_max and process-noise variance inflated by tau_max/tau_min. Garcia Crespillo, Joerger, Langel arXiv 2009.09495 (2020) gives tighter stationary and non-stationary bounds [V abstract]. Langel et al. ION GNSS+ 2017: CDF overbounding fails under time correlation; designing the filter with maximum-correlation models is not sound [S].
- Gallon, Khanafseh, Pervan, PSD bounding for stationary and nonstationary time-correlated errors, NAVIGATION 2025 + fault-free recursive ARAIM assessment [S]. Zhang & Jiang, Electronics 13(22):4384, 2024: recursive covariance linking integrity risk to uncertain correlation time [S].
- Nam, Min, Kim, Lee (KAIST), "Kalman Filter Innovation-Based Optimal Integrity Monitoring Against Unknown Bias in Time Updates", NAVIGATION 73, 2026 (navi.769). [V abstract] Position-domain projection of the innovation is NP-optimal and equivalent to snapshot solution separation; 9.6-11.7x faster than full SS; 1e-7 vertical alert limit 48.3 -> 28.9 m vs range-domain innovation monitor (sim GPS/IMU); gap to full SS shrinks with redundancy and barometer.
- Heavy tails: Yan, Zhong, Hsu, "Principal Gaussian Overbound for Heavy-Tailed Error Bounding", IEEE TAES 61(1):829-852, 2025: bimodal GMM overbound preserved under convolution; >78 % lower mean VPL than two-step Gaussian on urban data [S]. Cauchy-Gaussian overbound arXiv 2601.07299 (2026) [S]. Non-Gaussian ARAIM jackknife arXiv 2507.04284: baseline VPL inflated >60 m by heavy-tailed Galileo SISRE [S].
- Vision/VO integrity: Honeywell patents on solution separation with vision sub-filters (US 11662472 etc.; US 2023/0062898 VO integrity with MHSS for UAS/UAM) [S]; NTNU/SINTEF learned hypothesis pruning for GNSS/IMU/camera SS [S]; SS-KFIM INS/GNSS/VO for UAM, GPS Solutions 2023 (s10291-023-01423-7) [S]; fish-eye SLAM integrity, NAVIGATION [S].
- Cellular integrity: Ding, Seco-Granados, ..., Wymeersch, "Improving 3D Cellular Positioning Integrity with Bayesian RAIM", arXiv 2408.04994, IEEE TVT (2025-26): factor-graph message passing posterior as Gaussian mixture, no exclusion, >50 % PL reduction in simulation [V abstract].
- Factor graph integrity: Xia, Wen, Hsu, "Integrity-Constrained Factor Graph Optimization for GNSS Positioning in Urban Canyons", NAVIGATION 2024 (navi.660, CC BY): switch variables + chi-square constraint; PL under single-fault assumption; UrbanNav [S].
- Particle filter / map-matching integrity: particle RAIM (Gupta & Gao, arXiv 2101.06380) [S]; Korean PF FDE PL ~30 % smaller than KF with overbound sigma (2006) [S]; Toledo-Moreo multi-hypothesis PF lane-level integrity [S].
- LEO integrity: carrier-phase RAIM with Iridium augmentation (Joerger, older) [S]; VT 2025 thesis: Xona Pulsar 258-sat standalone ARAIM PLs [S]. No Doppler-only LEO PL paper found.
- Standards: RTCA SC-159 ToR Rev 26 (June 2026) is GNSS-only (DFMC SBAS ED-259B/DO-401A with H-ARAIM and OSNMA, publication expected early 2027; joint SC-159/WG-133 CRPA effort; integrity with CRPA active is an open issue) [S]. ICAO treats A-PNT integrity as cross-checking sensors; ICAO C-PNT assessment runs to Q4 2032 [S]. SESAR short-term GNSS-outage work proposes integrity levels in terrestrial navaid MOPS [S]. No RTCA/EUROCAE standard for non-GNSS alternative-navigation integrity found.

## Topic 6 notes 
- Coloured measurement noise: Bryson & Henrikson 1967 (NASA NTRS 19670025416) measurement differencing keeps state dimension n instead of n+m and avoids ill-conditioned augmented covariance [S]; later work shows it is a special case of augmentation [S]; ISPRS Annals II-2-61 (2014): shaping-filter models mis-model pair-wise correlation when correlation is large [S]. No GNSS/SoOP head-to-head of differencing vs augmentation found.
- Update-interval rule: adaptive GNSS/INS patent family (Groves/QinetiQ era, CA 2410691, WO2001094971): pseudo-range/range-rate errors correlated with time constant inversely proportional to tracking-loop bandwidth (~1 s); choose update interval > correlation time, or model as states, or use a Schmidt-Kalman filter [S]. Matches round-1 9.4 bullets; source upgraded from memory to snippet.
- Schmidt-Kalman/consider filter: consider states keep cross-covariance with unestimated biases; reported more consistent and lower MSE than an ordinary KF when navigation errors are considered [S]; Mars-entry SKF for navigation biases (ASCE) [S]. No GNSS-clock-specific comparison found.
- GM overbounding: Langel et al. 2021 and VT 2020 "Overbounding GNSS/INS Integration with Unknown Gauss-Markov Error Parameters" (augment each correlated error as a GM state; use tau_max with inflated variance) [S]; see Topic 5.
- FGO vs EKF: Wen, Pfeifer, Bai, Hsu, "Factor graph optimization for GNSS/INS integration: A comparison with the extended Kalman filter", NAVIGATION 68(2):315-331, 2021. [V] Hong Kong urban canyon 2-D mean error: LC EKF 9.14 m, TC EKF 8.03 m, LC FGO 7.01 m, TC FGO 3.64 m. 1 s window 5.18 m; 30 s window 3.74 m vs 3.65 m full batch (256 s). Gains from re-linearisation and use of history against non-Gaussian errors; long windows can hurt when the noise model does not fit the current epoch. EKF <0.1 s vs TC FGO ~75 s for the dataset. Radar-inertial aerial study 2026 (arXiv 2602.04631): FGO RMSE < half EKF's only when disturbances move the state off the linearisation point; comparable in nominal conditions [S].
- No FGO-vs-EKF comparison for cellular SoOP navigation with per-tower clock states found (Kassas group uses EKF) [S].
- Innovation consistency: NIS usable online without truth (Chen et al. "Weak in the NEES?", arXiv 1807.08855: Bayesian-optimisation tuning on NIS) [S]; autocorrelation/Ljung-Box whiteness checks are practitioner guidance, not a published navigation study found [S].
- Adaptive noise: VB adaptive KF (Sarkka & Nummenmaa; Huang et al. IEEE TAC 2017 for inaccurate Q and R) is the standard reference; Sage-Husa covariance matching may not converge [S]; no real-navigation VB vs Sage-Husa comparison found.
- Robust estimation: Huber-EKF less sensitive to pseudorange biases (GNSS/INS) [S]; DLR ITM 2020 M-estimators vs LS and chi-square RAIM [S]; arXiv 2603.19640 (2026) tunes Huber scale and threshold from GNSS error statistics [S]; WVU ION GNSS 2017 Cauchy/Huber graph optimisation [S].
- Invariant/equivariant filters: Fornasier et al., Automatica 2025 (arXiv 2309.03765): all modern INS EKF variants are EqFs with different symmetry groups; compares MEKF, IEKF, TFG-IEKF [S]. Low-cost INS/GNSS: EKF convergence region shrinks to ~30 deg (1 sigma) initial attitude error with large IMU biases; invariant/equivariant filters still not globally convergent, coarse alignment still needed (arXiv 2310.10597?) [S]; Ouyang, Wang, Wu, arXiv 2311.07049: iterated Clifford-RQEKF "fast and global convergence without prior attitude" [V abstract]. Delama et al., RSS 2025: EqF jointly estimates GNSS time delay on two fixed-wing UAVs, stays consistent while EKF degrades with delay [S]. Scheiber et al. 2023 EqF multi-GNSS UAV [S].
- Particle/point-mass filters for map matching: LSVL (arXiv 2212.03581): point-mass filter on 6.17 km^2 converged after 27 updates, 12.01 m RMS after convergence [S]; SWA-PF (arXiv 2509.13795, 2025) semantic-weighted PF for UAV-to-satellite matching, converges within seconds [S]; Jurevicius et al. 2019 PF + VO 2.6x better than ORB-SLAM2 [S].

## 3. Corrections and upgrades to the first-round report

1. **Iridium chip accuracy (10.1, 14):** round 1 had "10 m static" (vendor) and "no price or accuracy found". The Aug 2026 RockBLOCK APNT datasheet gives field-test median 25 m at 69 N and 42 m at 19 N, timing stability <50 ns and average offset within 5 ns of UTC(NIST) over 60 days, 10 g, 1.2 W peak, receive-only [V]. Iridium PNT ASIC commercially available 2026-07-14 [S]. Price still unpublished.
2. **Transport Canada accuracy figure (10.4):** round 1 said the 2025 rules give no navigation accuracy figure. Standard 922.04 requires the RPAS to indicate lateral position within +/-10 m and altitude within +/-16 m in controlled airspace; 922.08 requires that no single failure takes the RPA out of its operational volume, with annunciation [V]. No GNSS-loss or integrity clause exists.
3. **Adding a source raises the protection level (16.7):** upgraded from snippet to checked, with the mechanism. Jia, Khalife, Kassas (IEEE T-ITS 2023): one SOP whose clock is not shared adds a clock state that absorbs its information (no redundancy) plus a new fault mode, so HPL and VPL rise; two or more SOPs on a shared clock lower them; UAV experiment confirms (HPE unchanged, HPL up with one SOP) [V].
4. **Jammer penalties (10.4):** "fines up to $25k for individuals and $10M for businesses" are administrative monetary penalty maxima (repeat: $50k / $15M). Criminal fines under s.10(1) for s.4 / 9(1)(b) are up to $5,000 (+/- 1 year) for individuals and $25,000 for corporations; s.9(2) interception offences under s.9.1 are up to $25,000 / $75,000 [V Act text].
5. **s.9(2) context (10.4):** add s.9(3) (s.9(2) does not apply to broadcasting, subscription programming or network feeds) and Regulations s.54(2) (only prescribed exceptions are safety/property, evidence, law enforcement, federal defence/security; none for research or navigation) [V]. Still no case law or ISED interpretation on sync signals; legal opinion recommendation stands.
6. **Live jamming in Canada (10.4):** "DND trials" refined: SOR/2024-7 exempts DND/CAF and contractors only under contract and supervision; RCAF jammed GPS at Wainwright in 2023 exercises; companies cannot test independently [V].
7. **Correlated-error fix (9.4, 11.2):** round-1 ideas (bias states, inflation) now have a cited rule: Langel, Garcia Crespillo, Joerger, NAVIGATION 2021 (tau_max + minimum initial variance / non-stationary GM) and the MITRE 2019 inflation by tau_max/tau_min [V/S]. Upgrades memory to sourced.

## 5. Judgement: would more searching help?

1. **Spoof/jam detection:** methods are saturated; flight *results* are not, because they barely exist publicly. More searching might add one or two 2026 papers; the reliable next step is reading the Sci. Rep. 2026 and NAVITEC 2024 papers in full (paywall/size blocked), not more queries.
2. **SoOP cross-check:** saturated. The field is KTH (Papadimitratos), Kassas group and a few smartphone theses; no products.
3. **Authentication/receivers:** saturated for OSNMA, SAS, CHIMERA, recovery. Independent CRPA numbers only exist in closed test reports (Jammertest participants, DND) - searching will not find them.
4. **Using interference:** saturated; it is a small literature dominated by localisation, not navigation.
5. **Integrity:** saturated for the core (Jia 2023, Langel 2021, PGO, KAIST 2026). Vision/map-matching integrity is mostly patents; more search would add patents, not numbers.
6. **Estimation:** near saturation; textbook-level methods are covered. Diminishing returns.
7. **Planning:** thin literature; 2-4 more searches might add a paper or two, unlikely to change the picture (no paper quantifies pre-denial manoeuvres).
8. **Cooperative:** saturated for receive-only options; all strong performers need operator beacons (NET) or TX.
9. **Timing:** saturated for specs; prices require vendor quotes, not search.
10. **Legal:** primary texts now read; no case law exists to find. Next step is a lawyer and ISED, not search. Check Ottawa-area GPS interference exercise notices in the CFS/NOTAMs.
11. **Market:** news keeps appearing weekly (2026 funding rounds), so never strictly saturated; product accuracy and price data are not public and further searching will not produce them.
12. **Patents:** a proper landscape needs a patent database query (Espacenet/Google Patents with CPC G01S19/21, G01S5/02), not web search; FTO needs counsel.
13. **Validation:** near saturation; the useful standards (DHS framework v2 test scenarios, IEEE P1952 test plans) are not public in detail.
